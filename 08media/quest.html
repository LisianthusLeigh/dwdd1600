The Quest

A favor for a favor, repay your debt or pay the price

Making the Deal

Babushka Druadel is a Hag, an Arch Bheur Hag to be specific, but she is not as cruel or tricky as her sister hags –at least, not on the surface. She is known to grant favors or gifts to those in need, and many desperate people seek her out to fix their issues. She will do so, granted that the one she helps makes a deal: A favor for a favor. But deals amongst fey are far more powerful than a simple promise made on one's word –no, fey pacts are nigh unbreakable, and have disastrous consequences if gone against.

Each player character who joins at the start of this campaign must have, at some point in their backstory, made a deal with babushka. Since she is rather powerful, this deal can be almost anything. In the original campaign this is based on, Babushka erased all memory of the player character so she could avoid being executed for the murder of her mother. The favor they gain from babushka can be anything, but it is usually better if it is something grand or serious. The favor can also be granted at just about any time; Days, weeks, years, or even centuries before the beginning of the campaign are all times that are valid. This favor may also impact the mechanics of the player character, at the GM’s discretion. Below are listed a few favors babushka might have done for the PC.

  1. Babushka appears to the PC right as a loved one of theirs dies, or is close to dying, and offers to save them. She will heal or resurrect the loved one to their original state, and granted that they are not immediately endangered again, will live for a long while more.
  2. Babushka finds a PC who has been scorned greatly by someone in power, and offers vengeance against them. She will do one of three things, depending on the PC’s will. She will kill this person. She will humiliate them until they are outcast by all their peers. Or she will remove the memory of them from all documentation and minds, effectively stripping their titles and powers and leaving them fully, utterly alone.
  3. Babushka finds a PC who wishes for fame, and offers them a place amongst the stars. She will alter memories of the masses and gift grand clothes and equipment so that the PC appears to be a person of great fame.
  4. Babushka finds a PC who is greatly impoverished, and offers them the means to live comfortably with their family for the rest of their life. She will give the PC a pouch that acts as a bag of holding and will produce 25 gold coins a day, allowing the PC to have a large allowance of money per day.
  5. Babushka finds a PC who is on the run from something, who needs somewhere safe to stay. She will do 1 of 3 things, depending on the will of the PC. She will erase all memory of the PC from the public and from documentation, leaving them nothing but a rumor. She will kill those hunting the PC in ways that are easily dismissable as accidents. Or, she will grant safe passage to the PC to a land that is peaceable and safe from their pursuers.
  6. Babushka finds a PC who seeks to have a very particular skill or ability, and offers to grant it to them instantaneously. Depending on the ability, babushka will either magically grant them knowledge which allows them to perform the skill or grants them an item that will perform the skill for them.
  7. Babushka finds a PC who wishes to be physically changed in some manner, whether it be to be more attractive, have better physique, or otherwise. She will essentially cast the spell True Polymorph and give them their ideal form.
  8. Babushka finds a PC who is lonely, wishing for company in any form they can get it. Depending on the will of the PC, babushka will do many different things. She might summon for the PC a magical animal companion. She might give the PC magical assistance in confidence and charm so they might win over people where they are. She might even take them in as a ward, offering to teach them magic and treating them like a grandchild. That particular deal comes with far more consequences than the others, as babushka would have particular sway over this character and the character would have sway over her

Breaking the Pact:

Fey pacts are very particular, and as diverse and wild as the fey folk themselves. One thing is always true, and that is that breaking a fey pact has disastrous consequences. The severity of these consequences depend on the nature of the pact that is broken, though usually at least involved the boon being forcefully taken away. For instance, a character who has bargained for the resurrection of their loved one from babushka, if they were to break the pact or refuse to fulfill their side of the bargain, their loved one would once more die. The consequences are ultimately up to the fey with whom the pact was made, and, of course, the GM.

Starting the Quest

The campaign starts, truly starts, with every player character being summoned to babushka’s cabin in the woods. This can be via letter, via message, or straight up teleportation, depending on where the PC is at the time they are called. They arrive one by one to the cabin, and babushka hosts them for a while, making them wait to hear what she is asking of them. The PCs can mingle and introduce themselves before Babushka will present them with their quest.

Babushka gives the players each a magical charm, enchanted by her, that protects them from a fey’s ability to steal names– but it will only protect them once. She also gives them a bag of holding, which shares a pocket dimension with her own bag of holding. This allows players to send items to her through the bag as well as communicate via letter through the bag. Then she gives them the List and the Map, both are enchanted to update automatically to any changes made to the landscape or the list.

Once her gifts are given, she ushers the newly formed party out of the door and into the winter court of the feywild. The moment all of them are out, the cabin will seem to disappear, as though it was never there in the first place.

The Mechanics

Hour Tokens
Disclaimer: This is an optional mechanic but it serves to give incentive for players to not dilly-dally. If your players do not need such an incentive, this mechanic is not integral to the story.
Babushka gives the party 2 weeks to complete her grocery list, but the feywild’s time is all sorts of topsy-turvy, so the time limit is based on the material plane. Players can keep track of their time using Hour Tokens, which can be anything you have 336 of, where a token is taken for every hour spent. This mostly comes into play with travel and rests, where at least 1 hour token must be spent for a Short Rest and 4-8 tokens spent for a Long Rest (depending on the PC’s requirements, as Elves and Warforged are not the same, see DnD’s Rules for more).
Another optional mechanic with these tokens is that of a twist/dread system (inspired by Legends of Avantris), where players can expend 2 hour tokens to reroll a d20. The DM can also return 2 hour tokens to reroll a d20 as well.
The Maps
The feywild is strange in many ways, perhaps the most odd and confusing aspect being that places do not stay in the same spot. Two cities might be a days walk from each other one day, and a month's travel the next. So how do you navigate a land as inconsistent as this? You use a map, a magical one that is capable of keeping up with the constant changes.
The map is made of various hexagon tiles, there are Location Tiles and Terrain Tiles. Location tiles are places of note, such as a town, prominent feature, building, etc. These location tiles are where most of the “action” happens, where the items are located and where the plot truly progresses. The Terrain Tiles are for travel purposes, these locations are not all in close proximity, and these terrain tiles serve to separate them through natural landscapes.
These hexagons must be used in a way that allows them to be moved, as the map will shift every time your party takes a Long Rest (or at the GM’s discretion). The maps shifting is almost entirely up to the GM who embodies the feywild. They might choose to guide players in certain ways by making locations near or far. They might choose to infuriate their players by moving locations far away or impeding travel with location tiles. Whatever serves the purpose of the story is possible.
Traveling across tiles: Every time the characters travel across the tiles, there are two things that happen. The first is the Time mechanic, since they have a limited amount of time, travel is very important. Every tile has a certain amount of hour tokens it costs to cross, and players must ration their time to travel efficiently. Second is the encounter tables, every Terrain Type has its own encounter table, and every time the party crosses from one tile to the next, you roll a random encounter for the terrain they are entering. You may also roll an encounter when entering a location tile, but that is purely optional.
Travelling Across Courts: When the party completes the list of the court they are in, a portal to the next court opens somewhere random on the map (decided by dice roll or the GM). Players must travel to this portal, and while it only really appears for them, it is a portal anyone can use– they just won't be able to see it. Additionally, and most importantly, players level up when they move from court to court. The levelling system in this campaign is Milestone based, and while there are a few optional areas to level up in certain courts, the only guaranteed level ups are when the players travel from court to court. It is also recommended that you start your players at level 3, and scale down fights as needed.
The List
When the party enters a court, the magical list that babushka gave them automatically updates with the items needed in that court. The list is written in prose, so as to provide hints to the item and its location in a way befitting a fey– as confusingly as possible. Any spell casters in the party may expend a spell slot and spend 10 minutes to get a magical hint from the map. This hint may come in the form of answering a question about an item, telling the players which location it is in, or letting them know if they have indeed found the proper item.
