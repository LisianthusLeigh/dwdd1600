Harengon

The harengon are a fey species that is close to my heart, and the entire reason this whole project began. It all starts with Fergenor, for whom this site is named, who was my very first DnD PC, a Harengon Ranger. Still sort of unfamiliar with DnD, I decided on a backstory for him based on a few surface-level google searches into this strange "hare folk" that told me they were from the Feywild and that they were hunted by werewolves. From these two things, and only these two things, I began to create an entire world for my beloved character to inhabit.

The Hare Folk

Burrows and Clans

harengon live underground, under trees, in expansive tunnels called burrows. These burrows can range from simple holes in the ground to entirely furnished housing, all under the soil. They may build skylights to see above, or they may choose to dig further down to hide from the sky. These burrows are then connected and expanded over the generations, and many generations of a family will live in the same burrow.

harengon are also organized in Clans, which are groupings of 5 or more families (usually around 8) who live in adjacent burrows. These clans are named for the trees most common in where they settle, and they are run by a council made of the Matriarchs of each participating family.

Pine Clan (Winter)

The pine clan is known for their large population of druids and their love of bonfires. These hares usually love a good show and have a great sense of hospitality.

Juniper Clan (Winter)

The Juniper clan is known for their herbalism skills. Whether it be through brewing of various spirits (both in the alchohol sense and in the literal sense) or through their medicinal remedies, they are skilled at what they do.

Yew Clan (Winter)

The Yew Clan is known for their great craftsmanship, particularly carpentry. They craft the finest bows and arrows in the whole feywild, but they are able to build just about anything out of wood.

Magnolia Clan (Spring)

The Magnolia clan is known for their surprising skill in metal and stone work, due largely to their proximity to the Brigganoks (a kind of small, rodent like creature who mines gems) and they have a good eye for sparkly things.

Apple Clan (Spring)

The Apple clan is a clan of harengon that have dedicated themselves to the Princess of Spring, Verenestra, and act as her servants. In turn, they are never without food, drink, or merriment.

Sakura Clan (Spring)

The Sakura clan is one of the few harengon clans that have a thriving adventuring community. it is common for them to send their recently named clansmen into the wild to explore, learn, and return with new knowledge. As a result, the Sakura clan is very friendly and knows the most about the material plane of any harengon.

Oak Clan (Summer)

The Oak clan have a strong culture of fighting, believing that physical strength and prowess pave the way for a better world. They worship the lord of summer, Oberon, as well as Cerrunnos, the master of the Wild Hunt.

Aspen Clan (Summer)

The most secretive and anti-social of the clans, the Aspen clan is known for their incredibly well-kept underground libraries and affinity for stealth. Not much is known about them to the outside eye, and that is in part due to their association with He Who Hides. They give Him knowledge and in return he protects them, teaches them, and provides for them.

Jacaranda Clan (Summer)

The Jacaranda clan is the most relaxed of all harengon clans, believing in living one's life to the fullest and not worrying about what comes next. They are very friendly and trusting, and they throw the best parties.

Willow Clan (Autumn)

The willow clan was a clan known for their swimming and sailing, activities not common in the ocean-less feywild. However, they were all but wiped out by the Cult of Moonsblight due to their proximity to the temple. Now, the New Willow Clan, headed by Ammenya Katyana Radimova Henthorn Fergenoriel of the Fellowship of Nochlie, also known as "Kat", it is known for its strong sense of community and resilience. The New Willow Clan will take anyone in if they need a place, and so while the majority of the clan is made of displaced harengon, there are people of other species residing with them as well.

Cypress Clan (Autumn)

The Cypress clan is known for their mischevious nature. They do not wander far from home, but they know their home perfectly and use that knowledge both to help their clanmates through herbalism and also to play really funny pranks on anyone unfortunate to walk through their wood. As a result, they are beloved by the Spirit of Autumn, Inari.

Redwood Clan (Autumn)

The redwood clan lives in the traditional burrows, but they also live in the tree tops. They have special crafted shoes to allow them to climb easily. They are strategic, resilient, but anti-social. They keep to themselves, and do not venture out.

Traditions and Beliefs

Harengon are not traditionally religious, and if a Hare chooses to worship a deity it is usally one relevant to their clan (as shown in the clan descriptions). However, they are rather superstitious, and will often do things for the purpose of appeasing local spirits and asking for luck.

Harengon society is a matriarchy, where Women are seen as the primary choice for leadership roles. A lot of emphasis is put on the idea of Motherhood and relation between mother and child. As such, their word for "Elder" is "belegamme" meaning Grandmother.

Within the clans, the individual hares usually take on a particular role to help provide for their community. The biggest roles are as follows:

  • Forager - one who goes out to gather food and supplies for the clan, the most common role for one to take
  • Craftsman - also common, craftspeople of many mediums exist and create items of use or decoration
  • Digger - the equivilent of a builder, these hares are good at digging and expanding burrows
  • Bookkeeper - the rarest job, one that requires intellect, they keep detailed records of all comings and goings of clan members as well as are trusted with the Book of Names
  • Watcher - a guard of sorts, the kind that stays near the border to alert anyone of danger
  • Hunter - Harengon are vegetarian, so their hunters are more like warriors, seeking out dangerous creatures to stop them before they get too close. Not many hares have this skill or role

The Book of Names

Names are very important in the Feywild. They have power, they are tied to one's very soul. To be given a name is not a simple thing. So it is no surprise that Harengon treat the naming of their children very seriously. Names have to either be passed down or earned, and every single one of them has a particular meaning. Most hares go the route of choosing a name from what is known as the Book of Names. Whenever a named hare dies, they add their name to the book. And whenever a Penneth comes of age, they choose a name from that book. The harengon believe this keeps the soul alive, and grants the Penneth the wisdom of their name's previous owners. Alternatively, if a hare does something particularly remarkable, they may earn a name. For instance, the mother of Fergenor was named Henthorn, meaning Harpy Eye, because she spotted a large eagle who would have hurt the clan and got everyone to safety.
